Walk into almost any busy cafe in Kathmandu now and you will spot a little QR sticker on the table. QR menus went from novelty to normal in a few short years, and for good reason — they solve real problems that laminated menus never could. If you are still reprinting menus every time a supplier price moves, here is why a QR menu is worth setting up and how to roll one out without frustrating your customers.

## Why a QR menu earns its place

### It kills reprinting costs and delays

Every price change, every new seasonal drink, every item you run out of used to mean either reprinting the whole menu or crossing things out with a pen. With a QR menu you edit once and every table sees the update instantly. When your milk supplier raises prices before Dashain, you adjust the menu that morning instead of living with old prices for weeks because reprinting felt like too much hassle.

### It speeds up ordering

Customers can look at the menu the moment they sit down, without waiting for a server to bring one and come back. On a busy evening that head start on decisions moves your whole floor faster. It also frees your staff from ferrying menus around so they can focus on making and serving.

### It keeps the menu honest

A QR menu that is connected to your POS can hide items that are out of stock automatically, so nobody orders the momo you sold out of an hour ago. That single feature prevents a lot of small disappointments.

### It works alongside your printed menu

QR menus are not all-or-nothing. Plenty of Nepali cafes keep a few printed menus for older customers or for tables that prefer paper, and let everyone else scan. The QR is an addition, not a replacement — offer both and let people choose.

## How to roll one out

Setting up a QR menu is more about doing a few things carefully than about anything technical.

1. **Get your menu into a clean digital form first.** Categories, item names, prices, and a short description where it helps. This is the foundation — a messy menu online is worse than a tidy one on paper.
2. **Decide how prices are shown.** Be consistent about whether prices include VAT and service charge. Surprises at the bottom of the bill sour the experience; see our note on [VAT and compliant billing](/blog/vat-ird-billing-for-restaurants) for how to present this cleanly.
3. **Generate one QR per table or one for the whole cafe.** A single QR on a standee at the counter works for smaller places. Per-table QR codes matter more if you want table-linked ordering later.
4. **Print the QR clearly and place it well.** A crisp, well-lit sticker at a comfortable reading angle. Add a one-line prompt like "Scan for menu" in both Nepali and English.
5. **Test it yourself on a cheap phone and slow data.** Not everyone is on fast wifi. If your menu takes ten seconds to load on a modest phone over mobile data, trim it down.

## Keeping it current is the whole point

The failure mode of QR menus is neglect. A QR that opens a menu with last season's prices and three sold-out items is worse than paper, because customers assume digital means accurate.

- **Update stock status daily.** Mark out-of-stock items so they disappear or grey out.
- **Change prices the day they change,** not at the end of the month.
- **Retire seasonal items** when the season ends. A pumpkin-spice anything lingering into spring looks careless.
- **Check the link weekly.** Scan your own QR once a week to confirm it loads fast and shows what you expect.

> A QR menu is a promise that what customers see is what they can actually get. Keep that promise and it builds trust; break it and it erodes trust faster than a paper menu ever could.

## Pair it with eSewa and Khalti

The natural companion to a QR menu is a QR payment. Once a customer is comfortable scanning to see the menu, scanning to pay through eSewa or Khalti feels like the same motion. A few practical tips:

- **Keep your payment QR separate and clearly labelled.** Do not let customers confuse the menu QR with the payment QR. Different stickers, different spots, clear labels.
- **Confirm payment on your own screen** before clearing the table. A screenshot of a "sent" message is not confirmation — the money landing in your account is.
- **Record the payment method on the bill.** Whether it was cash, eSewa, or Khalti, tagging it as you go is what makes your [end-of-day reconciliation](/blog/closing-the-day-shift-reconciliation) match up cleanly.

Digital payments also leave a clean record, which makes your daily close and your tax reporting simpler. The QR menu gets the order in faster; the QR payment gets the money in cleaner. Together they take real friction out of a busy service.

## Start small and grow into it

You do not need to digitise everything at once. Start with one well-made QR menu at the counter, keep a few printed copies for those who want them, and commit to updating stock and prices daily. Once that habit sticks, you can layer on table-linked ordering and tighter payment integration.

A QR menu is one of the cheapest upgrades a Nepali cafe can make, and one of the few that pays back immediately in saved printing, faster tables, and fewer "sorry, we are out of that" moments.
